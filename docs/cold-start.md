# Cold start: how Encore grows when nobody's friends are here yet

This is a summary of research into how Partiful, Luma, Eventbrite, Facebook, Clubhouse, Superhuman, BeReal, Locket, tbh/Gas, Beli, Timeleft, 222, Pie, Meetup, Strava, Substack and Instagram got through cold start, plus Andrew Chen's *The Cold Start Problem*. The sources are web search results from November 2026, and secondary sources are treated as directional.

## What the evidence says

- **Guests turning into hosts drives growth in events.**
  - Eventbrite took off when attendees became organizers.
  - Partiful grew by word of mouth on a link anyone can answer without the app: about 500k monthly users, up 400% year on year (Sensor Tower, Q1 2025).
- **Come for the tool, stay for the network.**
  - Luma started as three pages a host could use alone.
  - Strava's tracking came before its clubs, and clubs grew to 1M in 2025.
- **Density beats breadth.**
  - Facebook went school by school, and half of Harvard joined in about two weeks.
  - About 40% of tbh's first school joined on day one.
  - Pie and Timeleft grew city by city.
- **Forcing people to grow the network gets punished.**
  - Clubhouse made inviting depend on uploading your contacts. It was fined in Italy and reversed course.
  - Beli's mandatory invites read as spam ("were you hacked?").
  - Growth spikes without a habit collapsed: Gas, BeReal and Clubhouse.

## Encore's plan

1. **A host gets value with zero friends here.**
   - One sentence becomes a finished invite page.
   - "Would you come?" and the date poll work with any friend, from a link, without the app or a code.
   - The live tally ("4 in · Friday is winning") is the aha moment, followed by locking in the date.
2. **Guests never need a code.**
   - Codes are for hosting only.
   - Guests RSVP, chat, follow and send kudos from the event link.
   - Their RSVP history follows them when they make an account.
3. **Invite codes are a gift, not a toll.**
   - Every member gets **10 codes**.
   - Hosts earn **10 more after each get-together they host**, once the date has passed and at least 3 people said yes, so made-up events don't count.
   - After an event, the host can **vouch**: send a guest who came a code with one tap. That guest skips the waitlist.
   - Everyone else joins the **waitlist**, and a member can invite anyone on it.
   - No contact upload, and no invites required to get in.
4. **Seed one atomic network before many.**
   - Hand-recruit 25 to 40 Founding Hosts in one neighbourhood and one scene: run clubs and the dinners after, in Brooklyn/Williamsburg, where our sample data already lives.
   - The target is one Encore a day, with guests overlapping across hosts.
   - Add host-to-host recommendations ("Hosts Maya loves") later.
5. **Strangers meet safely.**
   - Discover shows public events only.
   - Home addresses stay hidden until the host approves a guest.
   - Kudos are shown on hosts.
   - Discover stays quiet in cities without density.

## Metrics to watch

- **Host share rate:** share of new hosts who share a link in their first session. Target: above 60%.
- **Time to first share:** median minutes from signup to first share. Target: under 3.
- **Plan to locked event:** share of "would you come?" plans that become a locked event.
- **Guest to account:** share of guests who create an account within 14 days.
- **Guest to host:** share of guests who host within 60 days. This is the north star.
- **Invite claims:** share of codes claimed (watch whether this falls below 50%), and claimed codes that turn into a hosted event within 60 days.
- **Cross-host attendance:** share of guests who go to 2 or more different hosts in 30 days in the seed neighbourhood.
