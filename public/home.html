01 · The night

A good dinner takes a week.

  1. MON

    You post a date in the group chat. Eleven people react with a heart. Two reply.

  2. WED

    You ask again who's in. Someone wants to bring a friend. Someone asks for the address.

  3. FRI

    You buy the groceries and borrow four chairs from next door. You send the address again.

  4. SAT

    It's a lovely night. You do the dishes after everyone leaves.

  5. SUN

    You send nine payment requests and feel odd about each one. Someone texts "we have to do that again". So you start over.

02 · The problem

Most of the work happens where nobody sees it.

Supper clubs, run crews, pottery classes, hack nights and a baker's Saturday drop all have someone like this behind them. Most of them host after a day job, and many pay for everything up front.

The thanks scrolls away in the group chat. Asking for money feels awkward. Most event tools were made to sell tickets to strangers, not for someone who hosts the same twelve people every month.

03 · For hosts

What it does for hosts.

Hosts are the reason Pottery Lab exists. This is what it does for them today.

  1. 01 · Easier

    Less admin.

    • Write one sentence and Potto drafts the invite. Guests reply I'm in without an account and get a reminder the day before.
    • Every event has its own group chat. Spots, a waitlist and Ask to join keep the table the size you want.
  2. 02 · Paid

    Asking for money stops being awkward.

    • Set a price, like $45 a person. Guests pay through your own pay link. Venmo, PayPal and Cash App open with the amount filled in.
    • If you bake or make things, run a drop with pre-orders, a set batch and pickup windows.
  3. 03 · A following

    Guests become regulars.

    • The morning after, guests are asked for kudos. What they write stays on your page.
    • Anyone can follow you in one tap. Start a fan club, and members get your new events and drops first, with a chat of their own.
  4. 04 · Found

    New people find you.

    • List an event and it shows on the feed, on Discover and on the map.
    • Brands planning a team night find you by mood and occasion and ask to book. You accept, counter or decline.

05 · House rules

How we run it.

  1. Eight beats eighty. Eight people you'll see again are worth more than eighty you won't.

  2. Private unless you say so. Only people with the link see an invite. It shows on Discover only when you list it.

  3. No ads. We don't sell ads or your guest list.
