01 · The night

A good dinner takes a week.

  1. MON

    You post a date in the group chat. Eleven people react with a heart. Two reply.

  2. WED

    You ask again who's in. Someone asks if they can bring a friend. Someone asks for the address.

  3. FRI

    Groceries, a borrowed table, four chairs from next door, a playlist, the address again.

  4. SAT

    It's a lovely night. You're the last one up, doing the dishes.

  5. SUN

    You send nine payment requests and feel odd about every one. Someone texts "we have to do that again". You start over.

02 · The problem

The people who make a city feel alive are rarely seen.

Supper clubs, game and hack nights, run clubs, pottery and cooking classes, drops, walks, small summits. Someone hosts every one of them, usually on top of a day job, often paying for the groceries first.

The thanks lands in a group chat and scrolls away. The money is awkward to ask for. And the tools were made for selling tickets to a thousand strangers or planning one birthday party, not for the person who hosts the same twelve people every month.

03 · What changes

What changes when you host here.

Five things, and every one of them works today.

  1. 01 · Thanked

    The thanks is kept.

    • The morning after, every guest who was in is asked for kudos. What they write, and how many would go again, shows on your page.
    • Photos from the night land in your scrapbook, not in forty camera rolls.
    • Your stats count events hosted, guests welcomed, regulars and your hosting streak. Milestones show on the feed, with one tap to send kudos.
  2. 02 · Easier

    Less admin.

    • Write a sentence and Potto drafts the invite. Edit it on the invite itself, with the guest view beside you.
    • Share it as a link card, a flyer or a QR code. Guests can Invite friends from the same sheet.
    • Guests reply I'm in without an account, join the event's group chat, and get a reminder the day before by email or text.
    • Spots, a waitlist and Ask to join keep the table the size you want. A weekly or monthly event makes its own next date.
  3. 03 · Paid

    Asking for money stops being awkward.

    • Put a price on it, like $45 a person. Guests pay through your own pay link. Venmo, PayPal and Cash App links open with the amount filled in, and a Stripe link works too.
    • Run a drop: pre-orders with pickup windows and a set batch.
    • Your page can carry tip buttons, a supporter link and a wishlist guests can gift from.
    • Teams book your packages at your prices, with a deposit to hold the date.
  4. 04 · A following

    Guests become regulars.

    • Anyone can follow you in one tap, and the bell tells them about your new events and drops.
    • Start a fan club with a name and a few perks. Members get new events and drops first (24 hours by default), members-only events, their own chat and your updates.
    • You can see who keeps coming back, and invite the same table next time.
  5. 05 · Found

    The right people find you.

    • Your page wears your brand kit: your fonts, colours and logo.
    • List an event and it shows on the feed, on Discover and on shelves like "Good for going solo".
    • Bring past events into your scrapbook from Partiful, Luma, Eventbrite or a CSV, so your page starts full.
    • Teams planning a dinner or an offsite find you by mood and occasion, see the Trusted host badge once you've earned it, and request to book. You accept, counter or decline.

05 · What we believe

What we believe.

  1. Hosts come first. Guests second, brands last, and the order matters.

  2. Eight beats eighty. Eight people you'll see again are worth more than eighty you won't.

  3. Thanks should be specific, and kept somewhere you can find it.

  4. Getting paid for a good night is normal. It shouldn't take nine awkward requests.

  5. Private unless you say so. An invite is seen by people with the link, and shows on Discover only when you list it.

  6. No ads. We don't sell ads or your guest list.
