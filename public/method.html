Method

How Pottery Lab measures whether a field event worked

This is the full method. It's the same one the app uses, and the same one your RevOps team can rerun from the export.

1. Decide before anyone is invited

Before invites go out, you choose the question (sales-accepted meetings, or new opportunities), the eligible accounts, and a 90-day window. Pottery Lab locks all three. Nobody can stop the test early or move the dates after seeing results.

2. Hold out a random group

Pottery Lab assigns about 20% of eligible accounts, at random, to a held-out group that isn't invited. Tier A and accounts you mark as protected are never held out. Your CRO signs the split before it's locked. Held-out accounts keep their normal sales, email and ad coverage, so the only difference is the event. Lift is the gap between the invited accounts and this held-out group.

3. Check the sample is big enough

Pottery Lab calculates the smallest difference the test can detect, using the usual meeting rate for these accounts and the size of each group (a two-sided test at 95% confidence and 80% power). If that difference is larger than any realistic effect, Pottery Lab tells you before you start and suggests pooling the same format across cities and quarters.

Example. With a 10% usual meeting rate and 270 eligible accounts, a test can only detect a jump of about 13 points. To detect 10% → 15%, you need about 1,760 eligible accounts. At that point Pottery Lab recommends pooling, or reporting results without calling them lift.

4. Count pipeline in three fixed buckets

BucketDefinition
Created afterA new opportunity at an attending account, created within 90 days after the event, with a guest on the deal or confirmed by RevOps.
Existing deals with guestsAn opportunity open before the event, with a guest on it, that moved stage within 90 days.
MeetingsAttending accounts with a sales-accepted meeting and no deal yet.

A deal matched by company name alone is shown separately and stays out of every total until someone confirms the guest is involved. No deal is counted in two buckets.

5. Keep the evidence types apart

Every meeting and deal carries how it's known: a logged CRM meeting, a call or email record, or an AI inference. The result is also shown with AI-inferred records excluded. If the two disagree, trust the stricter one.

6. Report exposure, not just results

If a held-out account is invited anyway, referred by a guest, or contacted by an AE about the event, it stays in the held-out group and is logged as exposure. If more than a quarter of the held-out group was reached, Pottery Lab calls the result inconclusive and recommends a new design.

7. Write down the decision

The readout is one of: fund more, cut or change, or inconclusive. Each comes with the rule that produced it, the group sizes and the detectable gap. You record what you'll do (which format or tier gets more budget, which is cut, what to test next), and the next wave is checked against it.

When Pottery Lab refuses to report lift

  • No holdout was locked before invites went out.
  • The groups are too small for the difference to be read.
  • More than 25% of the held-out group was exposed.
  • Only AI-inferred records support the difference.

In those cases you still get operating results (who came, follow-up speed, meetings booked), labeled as such.
