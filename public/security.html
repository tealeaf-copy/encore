Security and data

What Pottery Lab stores, where it goes, and what we don't do yet

Written for the security reviewer who gets the vendor questionnaire. If something you need isn't here, ask us.

Where your data lives

  • Hosted by us, or in your own cloud. Pottery Lab is one Node.js service with a single database file. You can run it in your own AWS, GCP or Azure account (a Dockerfile is included), in the region you choose, so event and guest data never leaves your environment.
  • Workspaces are isolated. Each company's data is stored under its own workspace. Every read and write is checked against the signed-in person's workspace, including files, the audit log and the Claude connector.

What data Pottery Lab needs

Names, titles, companies and work emails of the people you invite; your event plans and budgets; and, if you connect them, accounts and deals from your CRM. Running an event needs no patient, health or payment-card data.

  • Call recordings are optional and governed by rules per market. You decide whether Gong, Fathom or Granola can inform guest lists (summaries only, full transcripts, or off). Every use is written to the audit log, and nothing from a call is ever placed in text a guest reads.
  • Guest pages are allowlisted. RSVP pages, the guest wall and public roadmaps only receive fields meant for guests. Internal notes, revenue, budgets and deal data are stripped on the server, not just hidden in the browser.

Access and accounts

ControlHow it works today
Single sign-onOpenID Connect with PKCE, signature checks on every ID token, and optional enforcement for your email domain.
Sign-inPersonal accounts with passwords hashed using scrypt; sessions can be revoked on the server; sign-in attempts are rate-limited per address and per account.
ApprovalsBudget and plan approvals are tied to named people and roles, reset automatically when the cost changes, and can require a different person for each role.
Audit logAppend-only record of approvals, AI runs that used call data, changes made from Claude, and admin actions. Exportable.
IntegrationsHubSpot, Attio and Salesforce (client-credentials connected app) credentials are encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) and used only for the workspace that added them. Claude connector tokens are per person, stored hashed, and revocable.
UploadsLimited to images, PDFs, video and plain data files, served with a restrictive content-security policy so an upload can't run code.

AI processing

AI features send the relevant event details to Anthropic's Claude API to draft plans, copy and follow-ups. Nothing is sent to a guest or an AE until a person approves it. If your policy restricts AI vendors, you can run Pottery Lab without AI features and keep the planning, guest management and measurement.

What we don't have yet

  • Compliance reviews: send us your security questionnaire and we'll answer it, including which audits and reports we can share. Ask us. If you'd rather keep all data inside your own environment, you can also run Pottery Lab in your own cloud.
  • SAML: not yet available. Single sign-on works today through OpenID Connect (Okta, Google Workspace, Microsoft Entra, OneLogin), and can be required for everyone on your domain.
  • HIPAA: Pottery Lab is not designed to hold protected health information, and we don't sign BAAs. Don't put patient data in guest notes.
  • Data processing agreement: ask us for ours, or send yours with the security review request.
